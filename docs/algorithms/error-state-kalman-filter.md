# Error-state Kalman filter (eskf)

**What it does:** estimates a body's motion from a 6-axis IMU (accelerometer and gyro): attitude, velocity and position in the world (z up), together with the gyro's and the accelerometer's biases, each with its uncertainty. It is the joint estimate that [[mahony-ahrs]] plus [[strapdown-inertial-navigation]] approximate with separate heuristics, in which one estimate can absorb another's signal: a rest test against the moving gyro bias learns a slow turn as bias, and the attitude carried on that gyro then leaks gravity, so a slow rotation drifts to one side. Here the gyro bias is a slow random walk (0.2 mrad/s per √s): following a 0.13 rad/s turn would take its σ days. Generic for any IMU.

**Inputs → outputs:** `New(DefaultConfig())` returns a filter waiting for its first rest; `Prior(&fit, σ)` seeds the biases from a multi-pose calibration ([[startup-calibration]], `calib.Solver`'s `Fit`) whose `Apply` the caller runs on the accelerometer's samples; the accelerometer's residual bias is known to the largest of σ (how far the caller expects it to have moved since the fit; an exactly determined fit's RMS is 0 and says nothing of that), the fit's RMS and 0.01 m/s². Held still or turned and held, 0.01 and 0.05 give the same estimates to a few millimetres. `Update(dt, acc, gyro)` takes the specific force (m/s², +g up at rest) and the rate (rad/s), sensor frame. After each call: `Q` the attitude [w,x,y,z] (sensor to world), `V`, `P` the world velocity and position, `GyroBias`, `AccelBias` (sensor frame), `Ready` (the first rest has set the tilt; nothing moves before), `Still` (quiet gyro and steady force), `Calm` (not translating). `Rejected` and `Clipped` count the samples refused (a NaN or an infinity, or `dt` not above zero) and skipped (a reading at `Config.AccelRange` or `GyroRange`: the sensor's full scale). `SetRange(accel, gyro)` changes the full scales in place (a sensor switched to another range, or a calibration's scale in use), no allocation. `Sigma()`: the attitude, velocity and position σ. `Reset()` forgets the motion and returns the biases to the prior; the next rest starts the filter again.

**Method:**

- Nominal state propagated per sample (quaternion, world acceleration, v, p). The attitude steps by the gyro's rate averaged with the previous sample's (a gyro reads the rate at the sample; that the MPU6050's gyro and accelerometer are read at the same instant through equal low-pass filters is an inference from its datasheet), and the sample's force is turned into the world with the attitude at the step's end, the sample's own; the covariance step uses that attitude too. Turned with the attitude at the step's start, a turn leaked g·ω·dt of gravity into the world acceleration: a turn in place with exact samples moved 2–17 cm. Body-frame references turned with the gyro (the rest test's force references, the shadow's gravity) turn by the half-angle rotation, which keeps their length; an Euler step grew them by √(1 + (ω·dt)²) a sample, 3.5 % of g through a 180° turn in 2 s, which the shadow integrated and gave back as speed at the next wake. Error state δθ (world), δv, δp, δb_g, δb_a: 15. Covariance stepped as Φ·P·Φᵀ + Q dt with Φ = I + A·dt, i.e. P += dt(AP + PAᵀ) + dt²·APAᵀ + Q dt, sparse, with A's four nonzero blocks (A[θ,bg] = −R, A[v,θ] = −[a_w + g ẑ]×, A[v,ba] = −R, A[p,v] = I). To first order alone (without dt²·APAᵀ, a positive term) P lost definiteness: the correlation matrix's smallest eigenvalue reached −0.2 over ten-minute runs and −2.6 in some uncalibrated ones; with it, it stays positive (0.0001 or more) in every run. The velocity and position variances are then inflated by a hair, ×(1 + 1e-5) a sample (0.1 %/s at 100 Hz): in long motion without a rest (vigorous shaking) the yaw, velocity and position errors grow nearly collinear (the yaw error times the acceleration feeds the velocity, which the position integrates; correlations past 0.9998), and float32 rounded that block indefinite (a recorded badge capture's 8 s of shaking: 101 samples uncalibrated, 521 with the badge's own calibration fit, first non-positive Cholesky pivots down to −31; the same propagation step in float64, from the same input, stays positive: 2.6e-5 against float32's −3.3e-4). The inflation pulls the correlations back: on that capture the smallest pivot is then 0.0010 uncalibrated and 0.0004 calibrated. Any inflation float32 can represent clears it (1e-7 and 1e-6 too; 1e-8 rounds to nothing), but the pivot it leaves is small: with 1e-5, 0.0004–0.001 on the capture and 0.00009–0.0025 in the test's shakes (1e-7 leaves 0.00003–0.00005 on the capture): a nudge, not a wide margin. The other tests' figures are unchanged.
- Updates are sequential scalar ones, each optionally gated on its innovation (χ², 1 dof), accumulated and injected once; then `guard` keeps the float32 covariance usable: a positive diagonal and each correlation within ±1 (without it P − k·phᵀ cancelled to rounding and squares closed metres away). The update is not in Joseph form (a Joseph-form update changed neither the eigenvalues nor the estimates: the propagation was what lost definiteness). A held state (the gyro bias in a violent tilt) is a consider state: its gain is zero, and the covariance takes the general update P − k·phᵀ − ph·kᵀ + k·s·kᵀ, consistent with that gain (the Schmidt form).
- **Tilt** (every `TiltEvery` samples): the force turned into the world against ẑ, two components, with the accelerometer bias in H. Gated unless calm: right after a rest the attitude is known to a fraction of a degree, so a stroke's horizontal acceleration is an outlier and the gyro carries the attitude through it (Mahony's fixed gain can't: it leans into every acceleration). In violent motion (the force off gravity by over 0.5 g within the last second) and after a clipped sample until the next rest, the gated tilt holds the gyro bias: violent motion is no evidence of it, and in a badge's vigorous shaking a few samples a second passed the gate and dragged the gyro bias by 0.08 rad/s through the attitude–bias correlation, against which the gyro never looked quiet again on the desk (no rest, the speed ran to km/s).
- **Looking still** (zero velocity may begin): *calm*, the world acceleration low-passed over 0.1 s under `StillAccel` for `CalmTime` (a rotation in place passes; the force alone can't tell: a horizontal acceleration changes |f| by a²/2g, and a smooth stroke passes a steadiness test), or a *long rest*, quiet with the force on a gyro-turned reference (a fixed world vector seen from the turning body) for `RestTime`, even when the world acceleration says otherwise (an unseen offset tilts it over the limit after a turn). A fast stroke's middle is calm only for a moment; a slow one's is not: 3 cm over 2 s peaks at 0.043 m/s² and stays under the limit throughout, at up to 0.028 m/s.
- **A rest begins** as soon as the body looks still, unless after a translation (the force left its turned reference) its speed is over `MoveSpeed`: a stroke's middle, while a pause's velocity error stays under it. After a turn it doesn't ask the speed (an unseen offset's leak runs it up), nor after `MaxGlide` looking still.
- **The shadow** ends a rest that is a slow stroke: the body's acceleration over the last second in 0.1 s slots, untouched by zero velocity, its velocity and displacement from rest at the window's start. Gravity is taken off in the body frame by a reference turned with the gyro (with its bias over 10 s, snapped to the estimate when that moves 2 mrad/s: the estimate wanders as a hand's tremor passes into it) and seeded at each rest from the force's gyro-turned 0.15 s average (a plain average lags a turning body). Taken off in the world instead, a 0.1° attitude error leaves g·δθ, as much as a 3 cm stroke's acceleration. Over `StillSpeed` it wakes the rest and gives back its velocity and displacement; then the body glides, and rest returns once the speed has stayed under `StillSpeed` for `StillTime`. It decides only while the body is quieter than that, 3× its rms at rest (learnt from the shadow of a second ago, so a stroke can't raise it before it wakes) under `StillSpeed`: a steady hand's tremor moves it as fast as a 3 cm stroke, so in a hand slow strokes are left to zero velocity as before; on a desk they register.
- **Zero velocity** gated, so a velocity the covariance says is real is kept, except while the body is still (quiet gyro, steady force: rest by its own evidence; gated there, a velocity grown from an unseen offset's leak was refused, the leak ran on and the speed sawed between 0 and 0.15 m/s after an uncalibrated turn); refused for half a second, it is taken with the velocity's variance reopened to its own size (a gate must not lock an error out).
- **Zero rate** (still: quiet gyro, steady force): the mean rate over 0.1 s, its noise the window's own spread (a hand's tremor is real rotation), gated so a slow turn stays out of the bias; an axis refused for a second reopens the bias's variance to the refused innovation (a warming gyro; about the vertical only the zero rate sees it).
- **First rest:** quiet and steady for `StillTime`, then `AvgTime` of force averaged: tilt from it, yaw 0.
- **After a clipped sample** the next rest re-levels: once still for `AvgTime`, roll and pitch are set again from the averaged force, the yaw and the biases kept; the velocity is set to zero (known to zero velocity's noise), the tilt's variance to the first rest's, and attitude, velocity and position forget their correlations with the rest of the state. A clipped sample's rotation is lost and its motion was outside the model, so the attitude can be off by any amount (tens of degrees after hard shaking) and the velocity is meaningless.

**The honest bound:** there is no position fix. An acceleration error δa (a tilt error δθ gives g·δθ) moves the position by ½·δa·t² until zero velocity: 0.1° is 1.9 cm over a 1.5 s stroke and 0.85 m over 10 s of motion without a pause. Paused shapes (strokes, squares with held corners) can be true; a long continuous motion's absolute path can't, for any consumer IMU. Uncalibrated, an accelerometer offset can't be told from tilt until a turn: a 45° turn with MPU6050-sized offsets (0.2–0.3 m/s²) moves 0.3–1.3 m (calibrated: ≤ 6 cm). A turn barely teaches the offset either: after 10 s still and a 90° turn the estimate is still up to 0.058 m/s² off a 0.04–0.06 m/s² offset.

**Known limit: a shaky hand at rest, uncalibrated.** Held still for 60 s in a shaky hand (an 8 Hz translational tremor of 0.09 m/s², rotational 0.027 rad/s; 10 seeds) the position drifts up to 23 cm with a multi-pose calibration's prior and up to about 64 m without one. The tremor keeps the body from looking still most of the time (calm about 15 %, against 80 % for the attitude-free still test), and uncalibrated the accelerometer's offset leaks into the world acceleration as well, which defeats any rest test built on it. The remedy is the calibration (`Prior`); a steady hand stays put (0.06 cm over 20 s). Turns, uncalibrated, move it too: the accelerometer's offsets, which the first rest folds into the level, leak into the world acceleration once a turn carries them off it; over 8 seeds a 2 s turn in place moves the body 5 cm (90° about the screen's normal) to 18 cm (180° about the right axis) on average, 30 cm at worst (with the force turned by the step's start attitude, 6 to 22 cm, 40 at worst). Calibrated, the same turns stay within 1 cm.

**Input contract:** finite samples at a steady rate (`dt` a sensor period, 0.01 s at 100 Hz). `Update` refuses a sample with a NaN or an infinity, or a `dt` not above zero (`Rejected`), and skips one with a reading at a full scale (`AccelRange`, `GyroRange`: by default ±2 g and ±2000 °/s, the ranges of the recorded shaking capture below (the MPU6050 itself powers up at ±2 g and ±250 °/s); a sensor set to another range is given it with `SetRange`; after `Prior` the full scale is taken through the fit, since the samples arrive through its `Apply`: (±range − offset)/scale, per axis and sign; `Clipped`). A refused sample doesn't touch the state; a clipped one is skipped and flagged: its rotation is lost, until the next rest the motion counts as violent, so the tilt holds the gyro bias while the body isn't calm (the hold's condition: not calm, and a clipped sample since the last rest or the force off gravity by over 0.5 g within the last second), and that rest re-levels the attitude. Without those checks one NaN made the state NaN until `Reset`, and one full-scale gyro sample left the tilt 107–114° off a minute later. A spike inside the range is a reading like any other: one 34 rad/s sample (±2000 °/s is 34.9) leaves a 26–35° tilt error, 105° after 3 minutes, and corrupts the gyro bias to 0.06–0.08 rad/s; `Reset` returns the biases to the prior, and 5 s held later the filter is `Ready` with its tilt within 0.5°. A gap is integrated as one step (a 1 s `dt` leaves 32–70°): after one, call `Reset`.

**Vigorous shaking:** a recorded badge capture shaken for about 10 s (44 clipped accelerometer samples), then 1160 s flat on a desk. Before the hold and the re-level, uncalibrated, the gyro bias was dragged to (−0.010, −0.038, −0.071) rad/s during the shaking, the rest test never passed on the desk, the speed reached 1091 m/s and the first rest came at 137 s, with the accelerometer bias then driven to (−8.6, 6.9) m/s²; the attitude-free strapdown chain is at rest at 40 s. The tilt update still runs every `TiltEvery` samples, ungated while calm and gated otherwise; what stops the damage is the gyro bias held by the gated tilt (the body not calm) during violent motion (after a clipped sample until the next rest, or the force off gravity by over 0.5 g within the last second) and the re-level at the first rest after a clipped sample. Now the filter is at rest at about 40 s, its speed under 5 mm/s, the gyro bias at (0.003, −0.003, 0.012) rad/s and the accelerometer bias under 0.2 m/s².
