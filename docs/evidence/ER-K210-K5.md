# ER-CAM-steps K5a — where the UnitV link loses bytes

K5 is split, because "why does it lose bytes" comes before "100 frames CRC-clean":
- **K5a** (this note) measures the link alone, without the camera.
- **K5b** applies what K5a finds and checks 100 frames CRC-clean.

Sensor: OV2640 at 0x30 (not used here).

## Check (written before the run)

A counter-stream probe (`tmp/k210/probes/counter`, stock `maixbit` target) sends 8-byte records `A5 5A <u32 counter> <u16 ~counter>` back to back. A tight Python reader (`tmp/k210/linkcheck.py`) counts missing counters and when they went missing. 60 s per configuration:
1. 1.5 Mbaud, unpaced;
2. 1.5 Mbaud, paced 256 B / 2 ms;
3. 115200, unpaced.

Pass = the probe reports exact loss counts and gap sizes for all three, and at least one configuration loses 0 records in 60 s. If all three lose records, K5a fails, and the loss timing is used to split further (host USB versus device).

## Result: **pass** (115200 loses nothing; 1.5 Mbaud loses bytes even when paced)

Reader skipped the first second after opening the port (the open resets the board, so the counter restarts). Logs `tmp/k210/logs/k5a-{u1500,p1500,u115}.{txt,raw}`.

| Configuration | Bytes/s received | Records | Loss events / 60 s | Lost | Loss sizes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.5 Mbaud, unpaced | 149,930 | 1,124,393 | 71 | 7,747 records ≈ 62 KB (incl. the restart gap) | 3–59 records (24–472 B), every ~0.4–0.6 s |
| 1.5 Mbaud, paced 256 B / 2 ms | 70,441 | 528,296 | 9 | 27 records = 216 B | 1–6 records (8–48 B) |
| 115200, unpaced | 11,602 | 87,014 | **0** | **0** | — |

What this says:
- **Rate-dependent loss.** The loss is on the link, not in the camera code: a counter stream with no camera, no encoder and no frame buffers loses bytes the same way. It depends on rate: at 150 KB/s about every half second, at 70 KB/s every few seconds, at 11.6 KB/s not once in 60 s.
- **Missing USB packets** [INFERENCE]. Paced losses are ≤ 48 B, under one FT232BM USB packet (64 B, 62 data). Unpaced losses span a few packets. That fits whole USB IN packets missing, or the chip's 384-byte receive buffer overflowing, when the macOS driver doesn't poll the device for a few milliseconds.
- **Not a device problem.** The device can't skip bytes: `writeByte` waits on the TX-FIFO full flag.
- **K2 run 1 is still unexplained.** It lost ~2 KB at 115200. That was not reproduced in this 60 s run at 115200. It came right after a flash, while the previous firmware's stream and ktool's resets were still in play; cause not proven.
- **No hardware flow control.** The FTDI's RTS and DTR lines drive the K210's reset and boot pins (ktool's `kd233` scheme), so RTS/CTS is unavailable on this board.

**Tooling fixes made during this step** (`tmp/k210`, not in the repo):
- A firmware that streams non-stop at 1.5 Mbaud made every ktool ISP greeting fail.
- `isp_quiet.py` parks the board in its silent ROM ISP first: it opens the port with DTR/RTS deasserted, runs the `kd233` reset, and waits until the port is quiet (1.3–1.5 KB trail).
- `ktool/ktool-flush.py` is `ktool.py` plus two local changes: open the port with DTR/RTS deasserted, and drain 0.3 s then flush before the ROM greeting. SHA-256 `d0afd4b8cd572d99669485e8344aa87414d9b492dacd3a26f672c6ab07f7dde9`. The pinned `ktool.py` (`800794f7…dca5`) is untouched.
- `flash.sh` runs both.

## Proposed K5b (next step, one check)

Two options for a lossless link, given that RTS/CTS is out:
1. **Stay at 115200:** 0 loss measured. QQVGA Y8 at 1.7 s per frame, QVGA Y8 at 6.7 s.
2. **Chunk resend at 1.5 Mbaud:** the host asks for a missing chunk again (`frame_seq`, `chunk_idx`) and the device keeps the last image's buffer. It adds a small host→device message to the frame format (additive in v1), and every image arrives whole at ~70 KB/s.

Recommendation: K5b = option 1 as the baseline check (100 frames at 115200, 0 CRC errors), then option 2 as K5c if fps matters for K6.

# K5b — 100 frames CRC-clean at 115200

## Check (written before the run)

Firmware `drivers/unitv/examples/frame`, QQVGA Y8 full field, 115200 baud, unpaced (`build/k2-full.bin` from K2, SHA-256 in the result). `camframe -baud 115200 -n 100`. Pass = `images=100`, `crc_errors=0 dropped=0 lost=0`; fps logged.

## Attempt 1 — result: **fail**

`build/k2-full.bin` SHA-256 `a334f40c8d8bcad997c558434ca89ea41681c21701fbd90fb5ab39a00daac1e2`; `camframe … -baud 115200 -n 100 -raw logs/k5b.raw`:

```
images=100 saved=100 fps=0.53 crc_errors=6 dropped=5 lost=6 text_bytes=17554
```

CRC-failed chunks in `logs/k5b.raw` (seq, chunk, bytes missing before the next magic): (0,0,3871) right after the port open, then (1,3,1743), (32,0,2581), (49,1,61), (75,0,>1459, a header lost too), (77,3,405). These are hundreds to thousands of bytes, i.e. 0.1–0.2 s of data at 11.5 KB/s, much larger than the ≤ 62-byte losses of K5a's paced 1.5 Mbaud run. So 115200 with the camera firmware and camframe is **not** clean, although K5a's 60 s counter stream at 115200 was.

Next: the same stream read by K5a's tight Python loop instead of camframe for 200 s (`logs/k5b-py.raw`). If that is clean, the loss is in camframe's read path; if not, it is below the application.

**Tight Python reader, same firmware, 200 s** (`rawcap.py 115200 200`, `logs/k5b-py.raw`): 2,210,411 bytes, **571 chunks, 0 CRC failures** (~114 images). So the 115200 link itself is clean, and attempt 1's losses happened in camframe's read path.

**Fix:** `cmd/camframe` now reads the port in its own goroutine (`asyncReader`: 4 KiB blocks into a channel of up to 4,096 blocks, honouring the timeout context), so PNG encoding, disk and terminal writes never leave the OS serial buffer unread. New tests (`async_test.go`, also with `-race`): every byte of a polled source is delivered in order, followed by the source's error; a Read blocked on a quiet port returns at the context deadline.

## Attempt 2 — same check, rebuilt camframe

```
images=100 saved=100 fps=0.58 crc_errors=1 dropped=0 lost=0 text_bytes=190   (logs/k5b2.raw)
```

**Result: the link part passes, but the check as written fails by one start-up chunk.**
- **The failing chunk:** the only CRC failure is the first chunk in the capture, at byte 36. The board was already streaming (booted by ktool after the flash). Opening the port reset it (macOS toggles DTR/RTS on open; [[ER-K210-M1]]) while that chunk was in flight. The stream then shows the boot line `frame: sensor OV2640 …` again and restarts at seq 0.
- **Everything after the reset is clean:** 100 images, 0 CRC errors, 0 dropped, 0 lost.
- **Frame time:** 1.73 s per image (0.58 fps) for 160×120 Y8 at 115200.
- **Fix for the next run:** to make the check pass as written, camframe should ignore what arrives before the first boot line after opening, or count only images after the first seq-0 restart. I have not changed the check after the fact; verify decides.

# K5c — chunk resend at 1.5 Mbaud (owner wants the stream)

## Check (written before the run)

Firmware `drivers/unitv/examples/frame` with `resend=1`, QVGA Y8 full field, 1.5 Mbaud, paced 256 B / 2 ms (defaults). Host `camframe -baud 1500000 -resend -n 100`. Pass = 100 frames saved and **0 lost frames** (`lost=0`, `gave_up=0`; abandoned images counted separately and explained), fps logged (from device timestamps), NAK count logged.

**What was built:**
- **Protocol (additive to v1):** a 16-byte receiver→sender control message, `"ERIN"` magic, frame_seq, chunk_idx, kind (1 Nak, 2 Ack), CRC-32 (`drivers/camera/frame/control.go`; `ControlParser` for firmware, no allocation).
- **Encoder:** `Encoder.Resend(idx)` rewrites a chunk of the last image byte for byte.
- **Decoder:** `Decoder.NextChunk` returns CRC-valid chunks.
- **Assembler:** `Assembler` rebuilds images from out-of-order chunks and lists the missing ones.
- **camframe `-resend`:**
  - Naks the missing chunks when an image's last chunk arrives, and again after 200 ms of silence.
  - Acks complete images; gives an image up after 10 Nak rounds.
  - fps comes from the device timestamps.
- **Firmware:** `frame` with `resend=1` keeps the image until Ack, or 1 s of silence.
- **Tests:**
  - Resent chunks are byte-identical.
  - Out-of-order reassembly with missing chunks 0, 13 and the last.
  - The control parser finds messages in noise.
  - An end-to-end camframe test with a simulated sender that loses a payload (CRC error) and a whole last chunk: 3/3 saved, nothing lost.

## Attempt 1 — result: **fail** (`gave_up=1`)

`build/frame-resend-y8qvga.bin` SHA-256 `f19a4a37acc8b3d0cd0838bc9bc79ab303df82301f457ba362cd8aafce362399`:

```
images=100 saved=100 fps=0.84 crc_errors=27 naks=132 gave_up=1 abandoned=3 lost=0 text_bytes=107640
```

- **What worked:** 100 images saved, `lost=0`, and 27 corrupt chunks were repaired by resend.
- **What failed:** 1 give-up and 3 abandoned images, from a host bug. The host Naks a missing chunk both when the last chunk arrives and on the idle timer, so a chunk can be resent twice. When the second copy arrived after the image was complete and Ack'ed, the Assembler started a new "image" of the same seq. It then Nak'ed chunks for that seq, which the device, already on the next image, ignored. The result was a give-up, or an abandon when the next seq arrived.
- **Fix:** the Assembler ignores chunks of the image it completed last. The unit test now checks that a late duplicate starts nothing and reports nothing missing.
