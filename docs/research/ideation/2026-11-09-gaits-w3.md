# W3 gaits: timing, footholds, swing curves, stability, servo calibration

**Brief:** `~/src/docs/tasks/ER-research-2.md`. **Questions:** EasyRobot `~/src/docs/evidence/ER-R2.md` §9 (W3) and [[W3-legged-ik]].

Builds on ER-R2-ik-review (evidence: `~/src/docs/evidence/ER-R2-ik-review.md`, outside the repo): FK and the geometric Jacobian are correct; IK converges from warm starts, fails for more than 3 joints, and the analytic leg IK is silently wrong behind the hip yaw axis. This note doesn't repeat that review.

**Status:** hypotheses and recommendations. Nothing was built or run.

**Labels:** observed, reported, inferred, `#verify`. The classic references below (McGhee & Frank 1968; Messuri & Klein 1985; Raibert 1986; Ijspeert 2008; Flash & Hogan 1985) are cited from memory and were not re-read this session.

**Network:** none for this note.

## Verdict

- **Timing:** a phase scheduler with blended phase offsets. It behaves like a CPG for speed and gait changes, at the cost of one add and one modulo per leg per tick.
- **Footholds:** neutral stance + velocity offset (Raibert-style), clipped to each leg's reachable annulus. Turning in place and crab walking are the same rule with a rotational or lateral velocity.
- **Swing:** time-scaled from the servo speed limit, with minimum-jerk horizontally and a cycloid vertically.
- **Stability:** the static stability margin from kinematics is enough on flat ground without an IMU. For the quadruped walk, add body sway.
- **Calibration:** zero offsets come from least squares over 3–4 measured poses per leg; the joint sign comes from enumerating 8 sign combinations.

## 1. Phase scheduler vs CPG (Q1)

| | Phase scheduler | CPG (coupled oscillators, e.g. Hopf/Kuramoto; Ijspeert 2008 review) |
|---|---|---|
| State | one global phase φ ∈ [0,1); per-leg offset Δᵢ; duty factor β | one oscillator per leg (2 floats) + coupling weights |
| Per tick | φ += f·dt; φᵢ = (φ + Δᵢ) mod 1; stance if φᵢ < β | integrate N coupled ODEs (Euler: ~10 flops per leg) |
| Speed change | change f (cycle frequency) and stride length; phases stay consistent | change the oscillator frequency; it converges smoothly |
| Gait change (tripod → ripple → wave) | **blend** Δᵢ and β linearly over one cycle (inferred); the stance/swing logic stays unchanged | change the coupling matrix and wait for convergence (a few cycles) |
| Reflexes (stumble, contact) | explicit: hold φᵢ, re-plan the swing | natural: perturb the oscillator state |
| ESP32 cost (18 joints, 50 Hz) | negligible | negligible |
| Debuggability | high: phase is a number you can log and plot | lower: emergent |

**Recommendation: phase scheduler** (fills the stub `motion/gait/types/contracts.go:191`, T-IK-09). Keep a CPG behind the same interface as a later experiment, if reflexive behaviour becomes a goal (inferred).

**Presets** (inferred from standard gait definitions):

| Gait | Duty factor β | Phase offsets |
|---|---|---|
| Hexapod tripod | 0.5 | 0 for L1 R2 L3; 0.5 for R1 L2 R3 |
| Hexapod ripple | 2/3 | each side a 1/3-step wave; sides offset by 1/2 |
| Hexapod wave | 5/6 | each leg offset by 1/6, back to front |
| Quadruped walk | ≥ 0.75 | LH 0, LF 0.25, RH 0.5, RF 0.75 (lateral sequence) |
| Quadruped trot | 0.5 | diagonal pairs 0 / 0.5 (dynamic: not statically stable) |

**Speed → cycle frequency:** for stride length *L* (bounded by reach, §2) and body speed *v*: stance time T_st = L / v and cycle T = T_st / β. Cap T so that the swing time (1−β)·T stays above the servo limit (§3). Above that speed, step to the next gait with a lower β (wave → ripple → tripod).

## 2. Footholds, turning in place, crab walking (Q2)

For leg *i* with neutral foot position **n**ᵢ (body frame), body twist (v_x, v_y, ω), and stance time T_st:

**p**ᵢ* = **n**ᵢ + ½·T_st·(**v** + ω × **n**ᵢ)

This is the Raibert-style "half the stance displacement ahead of neutral" rule (inferred):
- **crab walking** is v_y ≠ 0;
- **turning in place** is v = 0, ω ≠ 0: footholds move along circles around the body centre;
- combined twists add.

**Reachability with a 3-DOF coxa–femur–tibia leg:**
- In the coxa frame the workspace at standing height *h* is an **annulus sector**: r_min ≤ r ≤ r_max(h), with the coxa yaw inside its limits.
- Clip **p**ᵢ* to that sector minus a margin (e.g. 10 % of the annulus width). The clipped amount shortens the stride for *all* legs, so the stance feet stay consistent and the body twist is scaled, not distorted.
- The analytic IK must return typed errors (T-IK-02) so the clip happens **before** a silent failure behind the yaw axis.

**Turning in place** puts the most load on the middle legs' coxa yaw. Check that ±½·ω·T_st·|**n**ᵢ| stays within the coxa range; if not, lower ω·T_st (shorter steps, higher frequency) (inferred).

## 3. Swing curve for slow hobby servos (Q3)

**Servo limit:** 0.1–0.2 s/60° means 300–600 °/s of joint speed at no load. Under load, assume half (`#verify` on the owner's servos).

- **Horizontal:** minimum-jerk from lift-off to touchdown. s(τ) = 10τ³ − 15τ⁴ + 6τ⁵ (Flash & Hogan 1985) gives zero velocity and acceleration at both ends, so there is no step load on the servo at lift-off or touchdown.
- **Vertical:** a cycloid-shaped bump z(τ) = H·(1 − cos 2πτ)/2, or a minimum-jerk rise and fall, with the apex at τ = 0.5. Lift-off is vertical-first: the horizontal motion starts after z reaches ~20 % of H, which avoids scuffing.
  - Bezier works too, but needs its control points tuned to avoid overshoot.
  - The current planner's clearance stacking bug (T-IK-06) has to be fixed first.
- **Swing time from the servo limit:**
  1. Sample the swing path at 20 points.
  2. Compute the joint angles with the analytic IK.
  3. Take the peak |Δq|/Δt per joint.
  4. If any peak exceeds the servo limit, stretch the swing time by the ratio.

  This makes the swing time *derived*, not guessed.
- **Starting values** (inferred, small hexapod, 50–80 mm legs):
  - apex H = 25–40 mm (the obstacle height plus 10 mm);
  - swing fraction 1−β = 0.5 for tripod, 1/3 ripple, 1/6 wave.

  Wave gets long stance phases and short swings, so it is the gait that runs out of servo speed first.

## 4. Stability without an IMU (Q4)

- **Static stability margin** (McGhee & Frank 1968): the shortest distance from the centre of mass, projected on the ground, to the edge of the support polygon formed by the stance feet.
  - Without an IMU, estimate the CoM projection from kinematics: body CoM + leg masses at their FK positions, assuming flat ground and a level body.
  - **Enough for the quadruped walk (β ≥ 0.75) on flat ground**, provided:
    - (a) the body **sways** toward the next support triangle before each leg lifts;
    - (b) the gait refuses to lift a leg when the margin of the remaining triangle is below a threshold (e.g. 15 mm).
- **Energy stability margin** (Messuri & Klein 1985): matters on slopes and with dynamic disturbances. Not needed without an IMU or slope sensing.
- **Trot** is not statically stable. It needs either an IMU or a speed high enough for dynamic balance. Leave it for milestone 3, as easyrobot proposes.

**Sway planner** (inferred): before leg *i*'s swing, shift the body over 0.5·(1−β)·T toward the centroid of the triangle that remains. The minimum-jerk profile from §3 is reused for the body position.

## 5. Servo zero auto-calibration (Q5)

**Unknowns per leg:**
- zero offsets **θ₀** (3 values);
- signs **s** ∈ {±1}³;
- optionally a scale per joint (the PWM-to-degrees ratio).

**Data:** command K poses **q**ₖ and measure the foot positions **p**ₖ. Measure by touching the foot to a printed grid mat, with a 3-D-printed jig, or with a camera and an AprilTag on the foot.

**Solver:**
1. For each of the 8 sign combinations, minimise Σₖ‖FK(**s**∘**q**ₖ + **θ₀**) − **p**ₖ‖² over **θ₀** by Gauss-Newton. The Jacobian of FK with respect to **θ₀** is the existing geometric Jacobian evaluated at **s**∘**q**ₖ + **θ₀** (the sign scales only the derivative with respect to **q**), already verified correct (ER-R2).
2. Choose the sign combination with the lowest residual; reject it if the residual exceeds the measurement noise × 3.

- **K ≥ 2** gives 6 equations for 3 unknowns. Recommend **K = 3–4** poses spread over the workspace, including one with the knee strongly bent, to decorrelate the femur and tibia offsets.
- **Expected accuracy** (inferred): 1 mm foot measurement noise on 60 mm links gives about 1° offset error. Repeat with K = 6 to confirm.
- **Output:** a validated `chain` config (T-IK-07) with offsets and signs, replacing the raw `dh.Config` and `planar.Config`. `CheckZeroPose` becomes `Calibrate(measurements)`.

## 6. Decision-tree gait selection (Q6)

Answered in [[2026-11-09-on-device-learning]] Part 3:
- trees choose the **gait mode** (tripod, ripple or wave) and the step-height class from IMU or foot-contact features;
- the phase scheduler and IK execute the choice;
- with camera features (W4/W5), trees can choose step height from obstacle features without a simulator, trained from logged walking trials.

## Smallest offline experiment (easyrobot, no hardware)

**`gait_sim_test` on the host:**
1. A hexapod model: neutral stance and leg geometry from the ER-R2 probes.
2. Phase scheduler presets (§1) and the foothold rule with reach clipping (§2).
3. Minimum-jerk/cycloid swing with servo-limited swing time (§3), and analytic IK.
4. Run 10 s of tripod at 0.1 m/s with ω = 0.3 rad/s, then a blend to wave, then 5 s of turning in place.

Assert at every 20 ms tick:
- the stance feet are fixed in the world (< 1 mm drift);
- every target is reachable with no IK error;
- every joint is within its limits;
- joint speeds are ≤ the servo limit;
- the static margin is > 0 (> 15 mm for wave).

**`calibrate_test`:** synthetic offsets and signs, 1 mm Gaussian measurement noise, K = 3 → recover the offsets within 1.5° and the correct signs in ≥ 99 of 100 trials.

Both run in `go test` with no hardware. They exercise T-IK-01/02/05/06/07/09/10/11.
