# W8 lidar: module identity, SLAM approach on small targets, bus format, tree features

**Brief:** `~/src/docs/tasks/ER-research-2.md`. **Questions:** EasyRobot `docs/evidence/ER-R2.md` §9 (W8) and [[W8-lidar]]. Its decoder failure analysis is not repeated here.

**Status:** hypotheses with one offline probe.

**Labels:** observed, reported, inferred, `#verify`.

**Network (read-only):** 1 search (`"55 AA" 0x23 0x10` lidar protocol) and 2 page reads.
- `github.com` (API): Vidicon/Mini-360-Lidar
- `tinygo.org`: `machine` reference for esp32-coreboard-v2, the UART section

**Probe:** `~/src/tmp/ideation/wpftb/crc.py`, run with python3 offline. It decodes the one published sample frame and tries checksums.

## Verdict

1. **The `xwpftb` protocol is the AliExpress "Mini 360 Lidar", PCB `T-WPFTB-V3.2.2`.** The match:
   - frame `55 AA 23 10`, 60 bytes, 16 × (distance, intensity);
   - start angle `((H&0x7F)<<8 | L) − 0x2000`;
   - reported as fitted to a 360 Smart Life S10 robot vacuum; a replacement-part listing calls it "LD06" (reported, Vidicon/Mini-360-Lidar).

   The driver name `xwpftb` most likely comes from that PCB marking (inferred). So the owner likely has **two** candidates in code (LD06 and T-WPFTB). The T-WPFTB driver *looks* fitted to captured data: W8 notes its hex-dump logging and comments citing a "README" formula. That is an inference, with no capture in evidence (`#verify` with the owner's capture or the driver's git history).
2. **The angle unit is most likely 1/64°, not 0.01°.** At 0.01° the formula can't exceed 245.75°, which is exactly the bug easyrobot found. At 1/64° the sample frame decodes to 349.2°→359.4°, and the speed field to 6.03 rotations/s (probe).
3. **115200 baud can't carry this stream:** ~200 frames/s × 60 B × 10 bits ≈ 120 kbit/s (inferred from the probe's samples per rotation). 230400 or 250000 is right; the RP2040 setting is wrong.
4. **The checksum is unidentified.** Neither a 16-bit byte sum (easyrobot's assumption) nor 12 common CRC-16 variants match the published sample (probe). Until captures identify it, **don't drop frames on checksum mismatch**; validate with angle monotonicity and range checks instead.
5. **SLAM on small targets:** host first. On an ESP32-S3, a **grid-based scan matcher** (CoreSLAM/BreezySLAM-style search, or Hector-style gradient matching) on a decimated scan fits memory better than ICP with a k-d tree. The repo's k-d tree is buggy for k-NN (W5/ER-R2), and the EKF-against-map needs fixed rays anyway.
6. **Bus format:** publish both **raw polar scans** (with intensity and per-point angle) from the sensor node and a **derived fixed-ray scan** (e.g. 360 × u16 mm) for SLAM, NN and tree consumers and small links.
7. **Sector minima are good decision-tree features** (8–16 front sectors).

## 1. Module identity (Q1)

| Candidate | Header | Frame | Baud | Points/frame | Angle | In repo |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LDROBOT LD06/LD19 | `54 2C` | 47 B | 230400 | 12 | start/end in 0.01° | `x/devices/lidar/ld06` (W8: VerLen and units bugs) |
| 3irobotix Delta-2G/2B | `AA` … | variable | 230400 (Delta2B guide, search excerpt) | — | — | DESIGN.md mentions it; no matching decoder |
| **T-WPFTB "Mini 360 Lidar"** | `55 AA 23 10` | **60 B** | 230400/250000 (inferred, §3) | 16 | `((H&0x7F)<<8 \| L) − 0x2000`, **unit 1/64° (inferred)** | `x/devices/lidar/xwpftb` |

**Sample frame** (Vidicon README; decoded by the probe):

```text
55 aa 23 10 | 6e 5a | 4e f7 | 16×(dist_lo, dist_hi, intensity) | d7 f9 | e9 0b
speed raw 23150 → /64 = 361.7 rpm = 6.03 Hz            (hypothesis: rpm × 64)
start raw 22350 → /64 = 349.22°   end raw 22999 → 359.36°  (×0.01° would give 223.5° → 230.0°)
distances raw ((hi&0x3F)<<8|lo): 865 878 895 1840* 1536* 401 390 373 360 349 335 327 316 306 300 290
  (* hi>>6 = 2 and intensity 0: likely invalid points; others have hi>>6 = 1)
```

- **Distance unit:** raw values 290–895 for a valid indoor scene suggest **millimetres** (inferred). Vidicon's comment multiplies by 0.1, which would make them 29–90 mm, implausible for most points `#verify` with a capture against a tape measure.
- **Angular step:** (359.36 − 349.22)/15 ≈ 0.68° per point, so ~530 points per rotation and ~3,200 points/s (inferred).
- **Checksum:** no match for the byte sum or the word sum over [0:58], [2:58] or [4:58]. No match for CRC-16 ARC, MODBUS, CCITT-FALSE, XMODEM, KERMIT, X25, USB, MAXIM, DNP, BUYPASS, GENIBUS or MCRF4XX (probe). The published bytes may be a transcription.
- **Owner check that settles it:** a label photo and a 5 s capture at 230400 and at 250000. With ≥ 20 captured frames, a CRC search (e.g. a reveng-style brute force over polynomial/init/reflect) identifies the checksum offline.

## 2. SLAM on TinyGo-class targets (Q2)

| Approach | Needs | Memory on ESP32-S3 (inferred) | Per-scan cost | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ICP point-to-line** | nearest neighbours: a k-d tree or grid hashing; good initial pose | scan (450 pts) + previous scan + tree: tens of KB | iterations × N log N | fragile at 10 Hz with poor odometry; the repo's k-d tree has k-NN TODO and wrong pruning (ER-R2) |
| **EKF against a map** (repo `x/math/filter/slam`) | fixed ray angles; a known or concurrently built map; ray casting for expected ranges | grid + state | N rays × ray-cast length | works with **fixed-ray binning** (adapter needed, W8); ray casting 450 rays × ~100 cells is fine on host, heavy-ish on MCU |
| **Grid scan matching** (CoreSLAM/BreezySLAM random search; Hector gradient matching) | an occupancy grid; decimated scan | 10 m × 10 m at 5 cm = 200 × 200 int8 = **40 KB**; scan 90–180 points | candidate poses × points (~100 × 180 = 18 k lookups) | **best embedded fit**; tolerant of mediocre odometry |

**Recommendation:**
1. **Host first** (Linux/RPi): decode → fixed-ray adapter → the repo's SLAM, compared with a grid matcher on recorded scans.
2. On the ESP32-S3, a grid scan matcher on 180 rays at 5 Hz.
3. Loop closure stays on the host.

## 3. Raw polar vs fixed-ray scans on the bus (Q3)

| Message | Size per rotation | Consumers |
|---|---|---|
| `LIDARScanRaw{angles 1/64° u16, dist mm u16, intensity u8, t0, dt per point}` | ~530 × 5 B ≈ 2.7 KB | recording, de-skew while moving, debugging |
| `LIDARScanRays{n=360, start=0, step=1°, dist mm u16 (0 = invalid)}` | 720 B | SLAM adapter, NN input, W6 trees, MCU links |

- Both are protobuf messages in `proto/types/devices`, per the owner's rule that protobuf is the contract.
- The rays message is derived on the sensor node by **min-range per bin**.
- At dndm's link-context cost of 4 B per frame plus payload, the 720 B rays message is what goes over constrained links (inferred).

**Baud check for the T-WPFTB stream:**
- 3,200 points/s / 16 = 200 frames/s;
- × 60 B × 10 bits (8N1) = 120,000 bit/s.

That exceeds 115200. So the `drivers/lidar` RP2040 setting of 115200 can't be right; use 230400 or 250000 (inferred, `#verify` by capture).

## 4. Sector minima as tree features (Q4)

**Yes** (inferred). Take the front 180° in 8–16 sectors. For each sector, the minimum valid range clipped to [0.1, 3] m, quantised to u8 (≈ 1.2 cm steps). That is 8–16 features, combined with camera features from [[2026-11-09-on-device-learning]] Part 3.

- Lidar near-range events (any sector < 0.15 m) double as **self-supervised negative labels** for the obstacle avoider. This is the W6 question "lidar near-range as label".

## 5. TinyGo `UART.Read` when empty (Q5)

TinyGo's ESP32 `machine.UART` documents `Read(data []byte) (n int, err error)` as "Read from the RX buffer" and `ReadByte` as returning an error "if there is no data in the buffer" (observed, tinygo.org `machine` reference). The docs **don't say** whether `Read` blocks.

By the ring-buffer design, it most likely returns `0, nil` immediately when empty (inferred, `#verify` in TinyGo's `src/machine/uart.go`). Either way, decoders should poll `Buffered()` and yield (`time.Sleep` or a channel), or use the interrupt-driven RX the changelog mentions. The host-side busy loop (`VMIN=0, VTIME=0`, W8) is the same class of bug.

## Smallest offline experiment (easyrobot, no hardware)

**Golden-frame tests for `xwpftb`:**
1. Add the Vidicon sample frame as `testdata/vidicon_sample.bin`.
2. Assert header, speed and angles under the **1/64° hypothesis**: start 349.22°, end 359.36°, monotone interpolation across 16 points.
3. Assert distances in mm, with flag `2` points marked invalid.
4. Make checksum validation **pluggable and off by default**, with a counter of mismatches.
5. Add a synthetic full-rotation generator in this format (1/64° angles across 0–360°, wrap at 360°) to test scan assembly.

**Pass:** one assembled scan per rotation with monotone angles and the expected point count. When the owner's capture arrives, it replaces the synthetic stream and identifies the checksum.

The fixed-ray adapter test (W8 TODO) runs on the same synthetic room.
